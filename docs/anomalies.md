# Anomaly detector

The anomaly investigator agent needs something that has already found an anomaly before it can explain one, so this repository has a plain Python detector in `energyops/anomalies.py`. No model is involved in finding anomalies, and nothing here decides that an anomaly is a fault.

The detector compares each meter reading with what the same meter did at the same time of day on the other days of the same kind, so a Saturday is judged against other weekend days and a Wednesday against other weekdays. The baseline is the median of those other readings. A reading is flagged only when it is far from the baseline in three ways at once: at least six robust standard deviations away, at least twenty percent of the baseline, and at least two kW. The standard deviation used is the larger of that time slot's own spread and a spread pooled over the whole period, because a slot with only three to nine comparable readings can look much quieter than it really is by chance. Consecutive flagged readings in the same direction are merged into one event, which carries its start and end, how many intervals it lasted, the most unusual reading with its baseline, and the energy difference over the event as a signed kWh figure. Readings with fewer than three comparable readings, which happens with a short history, are counted as unjudged and never guessed at.

What has been measured, on synthetic data only. Across 600 clean runs (300 seeds and two meters) it flagged nothing. It found every injected overnight spike of 8 kW or more on a site idling at about 20 kW, found 59 of 100 spikes of 6 kW, and found every sustained midday increase of 40 percent or more but only 46 of 100 increases of 25 percent and 1 of 100 at 15 percent. So it is built to be quiet rather than sensitive, which suits a tool whose output a person has to read, and it will miss modest changes.

What it does not do. The thresholds are not calibrated to any real building, and the synthetic noise is simple and well behaved, so real data will need tuning and probably more checks, such as handling holidays, seasonal change and a meter that was replaced. A change that lasts for the whole period looks normal because there is nothing to compare it with. It looks at one meter at a time and knows nothing about weather or occupancy.

The detector is exposed as the read-only `get_anomalies` tool on the energy MCP server, with the same required site argument and the same loud failures as the fact sheet tool, but no agent uses it yet. The next step is the anomaly investigator, which would take one event, gather the facts around it and write hypotheses with a stated confidence and a suggested check.
